What the audit checks

Each check gives PASS, WARN or FAIL against gates fixed in advance and printed in the report. Any FAIL makes the verdict FAIL; warnings alone make it CONDITIONAL.

Data integrity
Are prices clean and adjusted? Are delisted stocks included, or does the universe only contain survivors?
Look-ahead and leakage
Does performance collapse when every trade is delayed by a day?
Transaction costs
Does the edge survive realistic costs, and at what cost per trade does it disappear?
Stability over time
Is the result positive in most periods, or carried by one good stretch?
Overfitting
Is the Sharpe ratio still significant once the number of variants you tried is counted?
Drawdown
Is the worst loss inside your limit?
Exposure and concentration
Does the book respect your leverage and position limits? Do a few names or days produce the result?
Benchmark
Does it beat simply holding the benchmark?

The checks are open source: read the code and its tests on GitHub.

How an audit works

  1. You send two files

    Daily prices and the positions your strategy held, as CSV. No code, and no explanation of your signal.

  2. We agree the gates

    Your cost per trade, the number of variants you tried, and your drawdown, leverage and position limits. They are fixed before any result is seen.

  3. The audit runs

    All eight checks, with every input file fingerprinted so the result can be reproduced exactly.

  4. You get the verdict and the fixes

    A report you can share with investors, a written memo of what to fix first, and a review call.

  5. Re-test

    After your fixes, the audit runs again on the same gates.

  • If you prefer, the whole pipeline can run in your own Azure subscription, so your data never leaves it.

What it is not

The audit tests how a backtest was built, under the stated assumptions. It is not investment advice, not a recommendation to buy or sell anything, and not a forecast. A PASS means no disqualifying problem was found by these tests, not that the strategy will make money.
