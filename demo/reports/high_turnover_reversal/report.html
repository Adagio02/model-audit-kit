What to fix first

  1. FAIL Transaction-cost realism. Trade less often, add a turnover limit, or confirm that the assumed cost per trade is realistic for the size and liquidity you trade.
  2. FAIL Stability across time (walk-forward windows). Find out what drove the strong windows and whether those conditions can be expected to recur.
  3. FAIL Overfitting (deflated Sharpe ratio). Report the true number of variants tried, and confirm the result on data that was not used to choose the strategy.
  4. FAIL Drawdown and tail risk. Reduce position sizes or add risk controls until the drawdown fits the limit.
  5. FAIL Exposure and concentration. Confirm the leverage and position limits in the mandate, cap position sizes, and check whether the result still holds without the few names or days that drive it.
  6. WARN Look-ahead / leakage (delay test). Check every feature and join for future data (for example .shift(-1), or joining on the wrong date) and make sure each weight uses only data available at that day's close.
  7. WARN Value over a simple benchmark. Compare against the benchmark after costs and decide whether the extra complexity is worth it.

Evidence

How to read the checks

  • Delay test: a genuine signal loses performance gradually when trades are delayed. A collapse after one extra day means the backtest used information it would not have had.
  • Costs: the break-even cost is the trading cost at which the edge disappears. High turnover makes small edges fragile.
  • Deflated Sharpe: adjusts for how many variants were tried. The more variants, the higher the bar luck alone could clear.
  • Exposure and concentration: checks the book against the mandate's leverage and position limits, and whether a few names or days produce most of the result.
  • Time windows: a reliable edge should be positive in most periods, not concentrated in one.

Method and reproducibility

  • Weights are decided at the close of each date using only information available then, and earn the next period's return.
